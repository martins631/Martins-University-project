Background & Purpose

This analysis examines the first-semester academic performance of 28,810 students enrolled across 5 faculties and 30 departments of a science-based university. Covering Levels 100 through 500 and 713 distinct course codes, it represents one of the most granular institutional performance datasets assembled for a Nigerian university setting.

The overarching aim is to transform raw student score records into structured, actionable intelligence — enabling the university management, faculty leadership, departmental administrators, and individual students to make evidence-based decisions that improve academic outcomes, reduce failure rates, and optimise resource allocation.

Each section below defines a thematic cluster of objectives, the specific problems it targets, and the dashboard it should power.

The Core Problem

Nigerian universities routinely generate vast volumes of student performance data every semester — yet the majority of this data remains locked in spreadsheets, result sheets, and departmental records, never analysed beyond computing GPA and printing transcripts.

Institutional decisions on staffing, curriculum review, and student support are therefore made without evidence. Failure rates remain persistently high. At-risk students go undetected until it is too late. High-performing departments go unrecognised. Bottleneck courses that consistently drag down GPAs across multiple departments are never identified or restructured.

This analysis exists to close that gap — converting 233,017 student-course performance records into structured dashboards that give every stakeholder, from the Vice-Chancellor to the individual student, a clear, real-time view of what is happening academically and what action is required.

01
Institutional Governance

University-Wide Performance Overview

Provides the Vice-Chancellor, Senate, and Academic Board with a single, authoritative snapshot of institutional academic health across all faculties, levels, and student demographics for the first semester.

⚠️

Problem Statement

Senior management currently lacks a consolidated view of academic performance across the institution. Reports from each faculty arrive in different formats, at different times, making cross-faculty benchmarking and Senate-level decisions almost impossible without weeks of manual compilation.

Dashboard 1 — Executive Overview
OBJ 1.1

Institutional GPA Distribution

Establish the university-wide distribution of student GPAs on the 5.0 scale, identifying the proportion of students in each class of degree — First Class (9.0%), Second Upper (20.9%), Second Lower (26.3%), Third Class (16.6%), Pass (7.8%), and Fail (19.5%).

KPI → Class of Degree % by Total Enrolment
OBJ 1.2

Faculty Performance Benchmarking

Compare average GPA and class distribution across all 5 faculties to identify which faculty leads academically (FBS avg GPA: 2.76) and which requires the most immediate intervention (FET avg GPA: 2.31).

KPI → Avg GPA · Fail Rate · 1st Class Rate per Faculty
OBJ 1.3

Gender Performance Analysis

Examine whether significant GPA disparities exist between male (59.1%) and female (40.9%) student populations university-wide and within each faculty, to inform equity and support policy decisions.

KPI → GPA · Grade Distribution by Gender
OBJ 1.4

Enrolment & Attrition Profile

Map student population across the 5 levels — from L100 (6,750) down to L500 (4,825) — to quantify the 28.5% student reduction across levels and understand whether attrition is uniform or concentrated at specific transition points.

KPI → Student Count per Level · Attrition Rate
OBJ 1.5

Year-of-Entry Cohort Tracking

Link each level to its year of entry (L100=2025, L500=2021) to enable cohort analysis — tracking whether the 2021 intake is performing better or worse than earlier cohorts at the same stage of their degree programme.

KPI → Avg GPA · Fail Rate by Year of Entry
OBJ 1.6

Score Variability Across the Institution

Measure the spread and standard deviation of individual course scores across the institution to determine whether the university has a healthy distribution of high, middle, and low performers, or whether scores cluster unnaturally.

KPI → Score Distribution Histogram · Std Dev by Faculty
02
Academic Quality & Curriculum

Course-Level Performance & Curriculum Effectiveness

Evaluates the academic quality and effectiveness of individual courses and curricula at the department level, helping HODs and course lecturers identify which courses are well-calibrated and which are persistently problematic.

📚

Problem Statement

Course lecturers and department heads have no visibility into how their specific courses compare to others within the faculty. A course with a 34% failure rate (e.g. ABE207) may run for multiple semesters without anyone recognising the pattern, because results are reviewed in isolation rather than comparatively across the department.

Dashboard 2 — Course & Curriculum Analysis
OBJ 2.1

Course Failure Rate Ranking

Rank all 713 course codes by failure rate to surface the highest-risk courses across the university. Courses such as ABE207 (34.0%), PHY201 (32.4%), and CSC209 (31.7%) warrant immediate curriculum or pedagogy review.

KPI → Fail % per Course Code · Ranked List
OBJ 2.2

Average Score by Course & Department

Compute mean scores for every course within each department to distinguish genuinely difficult courses from those where poor performance reflects delivery issues, inadequate prerequisites, or assessment misalignment.

KPI → Avg Score · Score Distribution per Course
OBJ 2.3

Cross-Departmental Shared Course Analysis

Examine how shared courses (e.g. MTH101, GST101 taken by all L100 students) perform differently across departments and faculties — revealing whether poor scores in shared courses are universal or department-specific.

KPI → Avg Score in Shared Course by Dept/Faculty
OBJ 2.4

Grade Distribution per Course

Visualise the A-B-C-D-E-F grade distribution for each course to understand whether courses are appropriately discriminating between ability levels or producing a disproportionate number of failures or near-perfect scores.

KPI → Grade Distribution Bar Chart per Course
OBJ 2.5

Credit Unit Load vs. Performance Correlation

Determine whether higher credit-unit courses (3-unit vs 2-unit) correlate with lower student scores, to inform decisions about course load restructuring and whether heavy-unit courses need additional contact hours.

KPI → Avg Score by Credit Unit Load
OBJ 2.6

Semester Curriculum Completeness Audit

Verify that every department-level combination has the expected number of courses registered in the first semester, and that total credit unit loads are consistent with JAMB/NUC minimum standards (typically 18–24 units per semester).

KPI → Course Count · Total Units per Dept-Level
03
Risk Detection & Early Intervention

At-Risk Student Identification & Failure Pattern Analysis

Identifies students and groups most at risk of academic failure or withdrawal, enabling the Student Affairs unit, departmental advisors, and counsellors to intervene before the second semester begins.

🚨

Problem Statement

5,607 students (19.5% of the university population) are currently classified as having failed this semester. Without a structured identification and tracking mechanism, a significant portion of these students will carry over courses without receiving academic counselling, increasing their likelihood of eventual withdrawal or expulsion.

Dashboard 3 — Risk & Intervention Monitor
OBJ 3.1

At-Risk Student Identification

Flag all students with a GPA below 1.0 (Fail classification) or who have failed 3 or more courses in a single semester, producing a priority intervention list for academic advisors, broken down by department and level.

KPI → Count of Fail Students · Failed Course Load Distribution
OBJ 3.2

Failure Concentration by Level

Determine whether academic failure is disproportionately concentrated at Level 100 (first-year transition risk) versus upper levels, to guide where academic support programmes — orientation, tutoring, mentorship — should be targeted most urgently.

KPI → Fail Rate % by Academic Level
OBJ 3.3

High-Failure Departments

Rank all 30 departments by percentage of students who failed the semester outright (GPA < 1.0), identifying departments like Agricultural & Bioresources Engineering where structural or pedagogical issues may be systemic rather than individual student factors.

KPI → Dept Failure Rate Ranking · Heatmap
OBJ 3.4

Borderline Student Tracking

Identify students whose GPA falls in the 1.0–1.5 range (Pass class) — the population most at risk of dropping into Fail in semester 2. Tracking this group enables preventive intervention rather than reactive response.

KPI → Count of Pass-Class Students per Dept
OBJ 3.5

Gender as a Risk Factor

Analyse whether failure rates differ significantly between male and female students within the same department and level — to determine whether gender-specific support mechanisms (e.g. female STEM mentorship programmes) are warranted.

KPI → Fail Rate by Gender · Department Breakdown
OBJ 3.6

Multi-Course Failure Pattern Detection

Identify students failing 4 or more courses simultaneously — a pattern strongly associated with personal crises, health issues, or severe academic unpreparedness — to prioritise urgent welfare and counselling referrals.

KPI → Distribution of Courses Failed per Student
04
Student Performance & Outcomes

Individual & Cohort Student Achievement Analysis

Examines the academic performance of students as individuals and as cohorts, enabling students to benchmark themselves and giving departments the ability to track high-achievers alongside struggling students.

🎯

Problem Statement

Students receive their results but have no reference point for understanding how their performance compares to their peers in the same department, level, or faculty. First Class students (9.0%) are rarely recognised early enough to receive scholarship or research opportunities, while the bottom performers receive no structured feedback on which specific courses dragged their GPA down.

Dashboard 4 — Student Achievement Profile
OBJ 4.1

GPA Distribution & Percentile Ranking

Generate a full GPA distribution curve per department and level, enabling any student to understand their percentile rank — whether they sit in the top 10%, median, or bottom quartile of their cohort.

KPI → GPA Percentile Curve · Dept Median GPA
OBJ 4.2

First Class & High-Achiever Identification

Identify all 2,590 First Class students (GPA ≥ 4.5) broken down by department, level, gender, and faculty — to support early identification for scholarships, awards, academic prizes, and research assistant positions.

KPI → First Class Count · % by Dept · Gender Split
OBJ 4.3

Subject Strength & Weakness Profiling

For any student or student group, identify which specific courses contributed most to GPA elevation or depression — enabling targeted advice about where to focus remedial effort in semester 2 or during carry-over retakes.

KPI → Score Variance Across Courses per Student
OBJ 4.4

Class of Degree Transition Analysis

Map how GPA distributions shift from L100 to L500 — determining whether students who start strong in L100 tend to maintain their standing, and whether upper-level courses create a downward pressure on previously high-performing students.

KPI → Avg GPA Trend by Level · Class Mobility Rate
OBJ 4.5

Departmental GPA Benchmarks

Publish department-specific GPA benchmarks (top quartile, median, bottom quartile) so students can self-assess realistically within the context of their own programme's difficulty level — avoiding misleading cross-faculty comparisons.

KPI → Q1/Median/Q3 GPA per Department
OBJ 4.6

Score Consistency Index

Measure the consistency of each student's performance across their courses (using standard deviation of individual course scores) to distinguish students who are uniformly strong from those with extreme peaks and troughs that mask their true potential or weakness.

KPI → Score Std Dev per Student · Consistency Distribution
05
Faculty & Department Intelligence

Faculty and Departmental Performance Diagnostics

Gives Deans, HODs, and exam officers deep comparative intelligence about performance within and across their faculty — enabling informed decisions about curriculum, staffing, and academic support at the departmental level.

🏫

Problem Statement

Faculty of Engineering and Technology has the lowest average GPA (2.31) and the highest course failure rates in the university, yet without granular department-level disaggregation, the Dean cannot determine whether poor performance is isolated to one department (e.g. Agricultural & Bioresources Engineering) or is uniformly distributed across all five engineering departments.

Dashboard 5 — Faculty & Department Deep Dive
OBJ 5.1

Intra-Faculty Department Ranking

Rank all departments within each faculty by average GPA, fail rate, and First Class rate — enabling Deans to identify their strongest and weakest departments and hold targeted departmental accountability conversations.

KPI → Dept Rank within Faculty · Avg GPA · Fail %
OBJ 5.2

Department Enrolment vs. Performance Correlation

Test whether larger departments (e.g. Computer Science: 1,964 students) perform differently from smaller departments (e.g. Genetics & Biotechnology: ~659 students) — to evaluate whether class size is a significant performance driver.

KPI → Enrolment Size vs. Avg GPA Scatter Plot
OBJ 5.3

Level-by-Level Department Trend

Track how each department's average GPA changes from Level 100 through Level 500 — revealing whether a department's students improve with progression (as expected with survivorship) or deteriorate due to increasing course difficulty.

KPI → Avg GPA per Level per Department · Line Trend
OBJ 5.4

Faculty Gender Composition & Performance

Analyse the gender composition of each faculty (FET: 75% male, FHMS: 64% female) alongside gender-disaggregated GPA outcomes — to assess whether gender composition influences aggregate faculty performance metrics.

KPI → Gender Ratio vs. Avg GPA by Faculty
OBJ 5.5

Departmental Course Difficulty Profile

Map the average difficulty of a department's course portfolio — using mean score and failure rate per course — to quantify whether a department's overall poor performance is driven by its curriculum design rather than student intake quality.

KPI → Dept Course Difficulty Index · Heat Map
OBJ 5.6

Offering Department Cross-Impact Analysis

Evaluate the performance of courses taught by one department but taken by students in another (cross-listed courses) — to determine whether offering departments are adequately calibrating their courses for non-specialist audiences.

KPI → Avg Score in Cross-Listed Courses by Host vs. Guest Dept
06
Strategic Planning & Policy

Institutional Strategy, Resource Allocation & Policy Development

Supports long-term strategic decision-making by the university Senate and Governing Council — including NUC accreditation evidence, student welfare policy, staffing decisions, and inter-faculty resource distribution.

📊

Problem Statement

NUC accreditation reviews, university strategic plans, and annual reports currently rely on manually compiled statistics that are neither standardised nor reproducible. Without a structured analytics framework, the university cannot demonstrate evidence-based quality assurance to regulatory bodies, cannot benchmark itself against peer institutions, and cannot make the data-driven staffing or infrastructure investment decisions that improving academic outcomes requires.

Dashboard 6 — Strategic Intelligence & Planning
OBJ 6.1

NUC-Ready Performance Report

Generate standardised performance metrics aligned to the National Universities Commission (NUC) minimum academic standards — documenting First Class rates, pass rates, and course completion rates in a format suitable for accreditation and self-study reports.

KPI → NUC Benchmark Metrics · Compliance Status
OBJ 6.2

Faculty Resource Allocation Justification

Use performance data to objectively justify differential resource allocation — demonstrating that FET's higher failure rates (23.9%) support the case for additional tutorial staff, laboratory resources, or remedial teaching investment compared to FBS (14.4% fail rate).

KPI → Resource Need Index per Faculty vs. Performance Gap
OBJ 6.3

Curriculum Review Priority Matrix

Build a priority matrix combining course failure rate, student enrolment size, and credit unit weight to produce a ranked list of courses most urgently requiring curriculum revision — giving the Academic Board a clear, evidence-based curriculum review agenda.

KPI → Priority Score = Fail% × Enrolment × Units
OBJ 6.4

Student Retention Strategy Analysis

Model the enrolment attrition from Level 100 (6,750) to Level 500 (4,825) and identify at which level the steepest drop occurs — to determine where student retention interventions (academic counselling, financial support, mentorship programmes) will have the greatest impact.

KPI → Level-to-Level Retention Rate · Drop-off Points
OBJ 6.5

Gender Equity Performance Audit

Produce a university-wide gender equity academic audit — examining whether male and female students have equitable access to First Class outcomes, whether STEM faculties show gender-based performance gaps, and whether targeted gender equity policies are needed.

KPI → Gender GPA Gap · 1st Class Rate by Gender · Faculty
OBJ 6.6

Semester 2 Performance Forecast

Use semester 1 GPA distributions as a baseline projection tool — identifying which departments are likely to see the highest carry-over volumes in semester 2, enabling proactive scheduling of supplementary examinations and additional academic support resources before they are urgently needed.

KPI → Predicted Carry-Over Volume · Dept-Level Risk Rating
